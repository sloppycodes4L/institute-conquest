How the guide teaches

Seven rules every lesson follows, each from established research on how people learn rules and interfaces.

Learn by doingEnactment effect

Turn 1 has 7 Your-move steps and 4 Read steps. Rules the player acts out stick better than rules they read.

One idea per cardCognitive load, segmenting

Two sentences at most, one rule per card. Extra detail (like the reinforcement math) hides behind a Why? fold.

Whole before partsAdvance organizer

Lesson 1 shows the three phases at once, so every later step has a place to land.

Point at the thingSignaling, spatial contiguity

Spotlight or pulse on the exact control, with the words beside it, never in a separate help screen.

Just in timeNeed-to-know sequencing

Cards, the Standard, alliances, ambushes and the Siege are taught the first time they appear, not on turn 1.

Help fadesScaffolding, expertise reversal

Full guide on turn 1, hints on turns 2 and 3, then off. Too much help slows down a player who already knows.

Recall onceRetrieval practice

One question after turn 1 ("who wins a tie?"). No score, no penalty; pulling the rule from memory is what fixes it.

Player stays in chargeLearner control

🎓 toggles the guide any time. Any lesson can be skipped or replayed. Nothing forces a player through text they don't need.

Decisions before we build

These change the scope of .007. Each has a recommendation.

Timed online wars

A Read step pauses the player, but it can't pause a real turn timer, and other players are waiting.

Recommend

In wars with a turn timer, Read steps show as Your-move-sized cards with no dim and no pause. Lessons stay; blocking goes.

Where the first war happens

The guide could run in any war, or in a fixed practice map with a scripted opponent.

Recommend

Any war, plus a Practice war button for new players (Local, 1 AI, smallest map, no timer). No scripted map to keep in sync with the engine.

Who counts as new

The game has no accounts, only this browser's storage.

Recommend

New = no ic-tutor, no saved name and no past wars. Everyone else starts with the guide Off and sees one Tip pointing at 🎓.

How fast help fades

Fade too slowly and it nags; too fast and players get lost on turn 2.

Recommend

1 full turn, 2 hinted turns, then off with a Tip saying so. Mechanic lessons (cards, Standard…) still fire once each until the guide is Off.
